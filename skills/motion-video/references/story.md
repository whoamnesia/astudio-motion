# Story for motion films (adapted from the HyperFrames story guide, Apache-2.0)

## 1. The one thing
- **What it is**, in one line: "You can now ..." for a launch, the one idea for an explainer, what changed and why
  it matters for a changelog. A whole release ("2.0") is not one thing - take its headline.
- **The one thing to remember** after the film ends, in the viewer's words, not the slogan.
- **One viewer doing one real task**, from the moment they need it to the result. That task is the spine; one real
  piece of data (a query, a file, a prompt, a number) travels through it from hook to payoff. An explainer's spine
  is one concrete example worked through.

## 2. Three storylines, then one
Write three storylines that are genuinely different films (not three variations of the same cut), in words only,
each 3-5 lines: the hook, the turn, the payoff, the world(s) it visits, the signature transition. Show them and stop
until the person picks (a mix "A's opening with C's middle" is one storyline). No stops requested -> build the
recommendation and name the other two in one line each. State assumptions once: length range, aspect(s), music or
voice, paid items (voice, music) with a free option beside each.

## 3. The hook (first 1-2s)
The first frame is the thumbnail - never empty. Open on the most characteristic thing: the product doing its magic,
a provocative question in huge type, the problem in one image, a number. No logo-first openings for social; a brand
film may open on the mark only when the mark is the story.

## 4. Beats
Launch shape: hook -> tension (the old way, the pain) -> reveal (the product/feature) -> proof (it working on real
data) -> payoff (the result) -> claim (one line) -> end card. Explainer: question -> intuition -> mechanism step by
step on one example -> result -> recap. Each beat causes the next; lines read as cause and effect ("so...", "which
means..."), not slogans.

## 5. Worlds
Visit 2-4 "worlds" (the product UI, a macro close-up, a typographic world, a 3D stage, the real place) and enter
each through an object on screen (zoom through a button, an object becomes the world, carry across). Worlds never
crossfade into each other.

## 6. Copy and voice
Short lines (<= 7 words on screen), active, specific; numbers over adjectives. Read time = 0.5s + 0.3s per word.
Voice-over: write for the ear, 2.5-3 words per second; re-time scenes to the real VO timestamps; never rush a read to
fit a slot. Captions: 1-2 lines, <= 32 characters per line, word-level timing for social.

## 7. The brand
Logo, colours, fonts and icons are the brand's own files; never redrawn from memory; never a coloured square for an
app icon. Product UI is the real UI (screens, recordings, Figma frames rebuilt in DOM); a missing screen is a
labelled placeholder, said in the reply - never an invented dashboard.

## 8. Tells of a generated film
Dark void + floating small things; glows, orbs, particles as decoration; purple/blue/mesh gradients and glass cards;
cream paper + serif + mono labels + "01/02"; everything centred; everything fading/sliding up; text that moves while
read; titles that drift after landing; HUD/corner brackets; logo walls; a product shown as tilted mockups; idle
wobble; click rings; one ease on everything. See `<root>/reference/anti-slop.md`.
